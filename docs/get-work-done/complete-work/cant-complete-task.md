# What if I can't complete a task?

Add notes explaining the issue, change the work order status to show it's blocked, and notify your supervisor. The work order stays open until the problem is resolved.

Sometimes you can't finish work because parts aren't available, you need help, or you discover a bigger problem. That's normal. The important thing is to document what's blocking you so others can help resolve it.

## 💡 Tips for handling blocked work

- Document issues as soon as you discover them. Don't wait until later.
- Be specific about exactly what's needed to continue
- Take photos of problems to make the issue clear
- Change status immediately so supervisors can see the block
- Check back periodically on blocked work to see if it can be resumed
- Update notes if the situation changes or you learn more

## Common reasons you can't complete work

### Missing parts or materials

**Problem**: You need parts that aren't in stock

**What to do**:

- Request the parts through the work order
- Add notes about what's needed
- Change status to "Waiting on Parts"
- Notify your supervisor
- Move to other work while waiting

### Need additional help or skills

**Problem**: The job requires expertise you don't have

**What to do**:

- Add notes describing what's needed
- Change status to "Waiting on Assignment"
- Contact your supervisor to assign additional help
- Document what you've completed so far

### Equipment or tools not available

**Problem**: You need special tools or equipment that aren't available

**What to do**:

- List the required tools in your notes
- Change status to "On Hold"
- Notify your supervisor
- Check if tools can be borrowed or rented

### Discovered a bigger problem

**Problem**: You found additional issues that need to be addressed

**What to do**:

- Document the new problem with photos if possible
- Add detailed notes about what you found
- Discuss with your supervisor whether to:
  - Expand the current work order
  - Create a new work order for the additional work
- Update the work order status appropriately

### Safety concerns

**Problem**: Unsafe conditions prevent you from completing work

**What to do**:

- Stop work immediately
- Document the safety issue
- Change status to "On Hold"
- Notify your supervisor right away
- Follow your organization's safety procedures

### Access issues

**Problem**: You can't access the equipment or location

**What to do**:

- Note why access is blocked
- Change status to "Waiting"
- Contact whoever can provide access
- Notify your supervisor

**In practice**

- Technicians often continue working on blocked tasks instead of moving to other work, wasting time
- Work orders are frequently left in "In Progress" status even when blocked, hiding the real problem
- Notes are vague or missing, so supervisors cannot help resolve issues quickly
- Teams sometimes work around problems informally instead of documenting them in the system

---

## How to document the issue

When you can't complete work, add clear notes:

**What to include**:

- What you were trying to do
- What's blocking you
- What you've already completed
- What's still needed
- Any photos of the problem

**What good looks like**

**Poor note**
"Can't finish. Need parts."

**Good note**
"Spiral freezer SF-01: Replaced conveyor belt rollers 1-3. Roller 4 bearing is seized and needs to be drilled out. Need drill and bearing puller. Completed 75% of work. Freezer is safe to leave overnight but should not be operated until repair is complete."

---

## Changing work order status

Use the correct status to show what's blocking you:

- **On Hold**: General hold, waiting for something
- **Waiting on Parts**: Specifically waiting for materials
- **Waiting on Assignment**: Need additional people or skills
- **Waiting**: Other reasons (access, approval, etc.)

Your supervisor can see these statuses and help resolve the issue.

---

## What happens next

After you update the status and add notes:

- Your supervisor is notified
- They can see exactly what's blocking you
- They work to resolve the issue (order parts, assign help, etc.)
- You're notified when you can continue
- Work order stays in your queue until resolved

---

## Common mistakes

- Leaving work orders in "In Progress" when they are actually blocked
- Writing vague notes that don't explain what's needed
- Not notifying the supervisor, assuming they will check the system
- Continuing to work on blocked tasks instead of moving to other work
- Forgetting to update notes when the situation changes

---

## Why proper documentation matters

- If you do not change status, supervisors cannot see blocked work and delays get worse
- If notes are vague, resolving the issue takes longer because details are missing
- If blocked work is not documented, the same problems repeat on future jobs
- Poor communication leads to wasted time and missed deadlines

---

## Preventing future blocks

Help prevent similar issues:

- Review work orders thoroughly before starting to identify potential issues early
- Check parts availability before you begin work, not after you need them
- Verify you have the correct tools and equipment before starting
- Ask questions if instructions are unclear. Don't guess.
- Report recurring problems to your supervisor so they can be fixed systematically
