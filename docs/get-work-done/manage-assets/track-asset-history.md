## 💡 Tips for success

**For technicians:**

- Complete work orders promptly so history stays current
- Add detailed notes about what you did
- Record all parts used
- Take photos of problems and repairs
- Enter meter readings regularly

**For supervisors:**

- Review and close work orders quickly
- Make sure costs are accurate
- Verify meter readings are current
- Use consistent failure codes
- Keep asset records up to date


## Why track asset history?

Asset history shows you the complete story of your equipment. You can see what maintenance was done, when it happened, and what it cost.

This helps you:

- Spot problems that keep happening
- Plan better maintenance schedules
- Decide whether to repair or replace equipment
- Estimate future maintenance costs

Every completed work order becomes part of the permanent record. This creates a reliable history you can use to make smart decisions.

---

## What's included in asset history?

The system automatically tracks these details for each asset:

**Work orders**
All maintenance and repairs performed on the equipment.

**Meter readings**
Equipment usage over time (hours run, cycles completed, miles driven).

**Costs**
Labor, parts, and total maintenance expenses.

**Downtime**
When equipment was out of service and for how long.

**Failures**
Problems that occurred and how they were fixed.

**Moves**
Location changes and transfers.

**Specifications**
Technical details and attributes.

## When should I review asset history?

Check asset history when you need to:

- Understand why a problem keeps happening
- Plan preventive maintenance schedules
- Decide whether to repair or replace equipment
- Estimate maintenance costs for budgeting
- Troubleshoot current issues
- Prepare for audits or compliance reviews

---

## How do I view asset history?

### What to do:

1. Open the Assets application.
2. Search for the asset by name, number, or location.
3. Open the asset record.
4. Review the information you need:
   - **Work history**: See all completed work orders, who did the work, and costs
   - **Meter readings**: View usage trends and identify unusual patterns
   - **Costs**: See total maintenance costs and cost breakdown

## How do I use history to make decisions?

### Identify recurring problems

If you see the same issue repeatedly:

- Review failure codes to spot patterns
- Check if preventive maintenance needs adjustment
- Consider if the problem indicates a larger issue
- Evaluate if replacement is more cost-effective than continued repairs

### Plan preventive maintenance

Use history to:

- See what maintenance has been done
- Identify optimal maintenance intervals
- Adjust schedules based on actual usage
- Prevent problems before they occur

### Evaluate equipment performance

Track:

- How often equipment goes down and for how long
- Maintenance costs compared to equipment value
- Reliability trends over time
- Whether equipment meets performance expectations

## What if history is incomplete?

If asset history is missing or incomplete:

1. Check if work orders were closed properly.
2. Verify the asset number is correct.
3. Look for work orders under old asset numbers.
4. Contact your administrator if data needs to be corrected.
5. Start maintaining complete records going forward.
